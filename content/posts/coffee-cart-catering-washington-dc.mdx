Coffee cart catering in Washington DC runs on two clocks: the building's and the agenda's. Security desks want paperwork days ahead, morning sessions start at 8, and many downtown buildings have little parking and tight load-ins. This guide is for DC event managers, executive assistants and conference planners who want a real espresso bar at their event without surprises at the loading dock.

**The short answer:** to book coffee cart catering in Washington DC, start four to six weeks out, send your building's security and insurance requirements with your first request, and plan for the crew to arrive about 90 minutes before service. Each cart makes 65 to 75 specialty drinks an hour, so add a barista or cart for big morning rushes.

## What makes DC events different

Most cities have offices, hotels and conference halls. DC adds a layer that changes how you hire any vendor: federal buildings, embassies, association headquarters and secure corporate floors. Each of those can mean a guest list for staff, an ID check at the door and a certificate of insurance written to the building's exact language.

The calendar is different too. DC's event day starts early, with breakfast sessions, Hill briefings and pre-market client meetings, and it often ends with a reception. A coffee cart that is still setting up at 7:55 is a coffee cart that missed the event.

## Security, COIs and clearance: a DC checklist

Give yourself more paperwork time in DC than you would in most cities. Use this checklist when you book.

- [ ] Ask building management for its COI requirements, including additional-insured language
- [ ] Ask security what it needs for vendor staff: names, ID, a manifest, or escort
- [ ] Confirm the delivery entrance, loading window and elevator
- [ ] Confirm the earliest time the crew can enter
- [ ] Send all of the above to your caterer with the booking, not the week of the event
- [ ] Share an on-site contact who can meet the crew at the door

Atelier issues COIs to venue specs and handles clearance paperwork and staff manifests for federal buildings and secure venues, given lead time.

## The early-morning timeline

For an 8am service, Atelier is usually on site by 6:30. Work backward from your first session like this:

| Time | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| 6:00 to 6:30 | Crew checks in at security and loads in |
| 6:30 to 7:30 | Cart set up, espresso dialed in, menu signage placed |
| 7:30 to 8:00 | First drinks for early arrivals and staff |
| 8:00 to 8:45 | Peak rush before the first session |
| Breaks | Short spikes, so keep the barista count up |

If your building does not open before 7, tell the caterer. They will plan a faster setup or move service to the first break.

## Staffing for DC's morning rush

Each Atelier cart makes 65 to 75 specialty drinks per hour. In DC, almost every event has its peak in the first hour, so size the bar for that hour.

| Guests | Morning peak | Suggested setup |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Up to 75 | Manageable with one barista | One cart |
| 75 to 200 | Busy for the first 45 minutes | One cart, add a second barista |
| 200 to 400 | Long line without help | Two carts, or a second station such as a matcha bar |
| 400+ | Expo or conference floor | Multiple carts across the space |

## Outdoor events: the Mall, parks and rooftops

Outdoor events on the National Mall or in DC parks are possible with permits and a power plan agreed early. Rooftops and courtyards are simpler, but still confirm power, shade and a level surface. In summer, lean into cold brew, iced lattes and the hibiscus refresher.
