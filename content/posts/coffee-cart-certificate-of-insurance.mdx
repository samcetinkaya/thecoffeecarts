A **coffee cart certificate of insurance** is the one document that decides whether your barista gets past the loading dock. Office towers, hotels, campuses and convention centers almost all require a vendor COI before load-in, and each writes its own wording. If the certificate is wrong, the cart waits on the sidewalk while someone calls an insurance agent. This guide is for facilities managers, office managers and event planners who have to request, check and approve that paperwork.

**The short answer:** ask your property manager for the building's written COI requirements, send them to the caterer when you book, and check the certificate against them before event day. Confirm the certificate holder, the additional insured names, the limits, the policy dates and any waiver of subrogation. A certificate alone does not make anyone an additional insured; the policy needs an endorsement.

This is general information for planners, not insurance or legal advice. Your building's spec, your company's risk team and the caterer's licensed agent have the final word.

## What a coffee cart certificate of insurance actually is

Most COIs you will see are on the **ACORD 25**, the standard Certificate of Liability Insurance form. The Hartford describes it as [a one-page proof of coverage form](https://www.thehartford.com/business-insurance/acord-certificate-of-insurance) that lists the named insured, the insurers, the types of liability coverage, policy numbers, effective and expiration dates, limits, and a description of operations or additional insured details.

Two things to know before you read one:

- **It is not the policy.** ACORD's own [certificates FAQ](https://acord.org/docs/default-source/forms/acordcertificatesfaq) says a certificate is not an insurance policy and does not amend, extend or alter coverage. Only an endorsement to the policy changes coverage.
- **It is issued by the caterer's agent or broker** (the "Producer" box), not by the caterer. A certificate that comes from the agent is easier to trust than a PDF the vendor edited themselves.

### Certificate holder vs additional insured

This is the distinction that trips up most planners.

| Term | What it means | What you need from the caterer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Certificate holder | The party the certificate is issued to, usually your building owner or your company | The exact legal name and address in the Certificate Holder box |
| Additional insured | A party added to the caterer's policy so it is covered for claims arising from the caterer's work | An "X" in the ADDL INSD column and, if your building asks, a copy of the endorsement |
| Waiver of subrogation | The caterer's insurer agrees not to seek repayment from the building after paying a claim | An "X" in the SUBR WVD column and the endorsement if required |
| Primary and non-contributory | The caterer's policy pays first, before the building's own insurance | Wording in the description box, backed by the endorsement |

Law firm Reed Smith's [guide to additional insured requirements](https://www.reedsmith.com/articles/navigating-additional-insured-requirements-in-commercial-contracts-a-practical-guide-for-businesses/) is blunt that a COI alone does not guarantee coverage, and that certificates typically say they confer no rights on the holder. It recommends getting the additional insured endorsement itself where feasible. That is why many property managers now ask for the endorsement page along with the ACORD 25.

Requirements vary by owner, but the published specs below show the pattern. These are real documents from venues in the markets this site covers; they are examples, not the rules for your building.

| [Vendor COI form for 1333 New Hampshire Ave NW](https://spia.princeton.edu/sites/default/files/2023-08/Vendor%20COI%20Requirements%201333NH.pdf) (Dupont Circle office building, DC) | $1M per occurrence, $2M aggregate, $2M products-completed operations | Auto $1M; $1M umbrella; employers liability $1M; property coverage for vendors bringing equipment; 30-day notice of cancellation | Building owner as holder; property manager and management company as additional insureds |
| [NYC DOT permit insurance requirements](https://nyc.gov/html/dot/downloads/pdf/insurancereq.pdf) (street and sidewalk permits) | At least $1M per occurrence | Workers comp per New York law; insurer rated A-VII or better; 30 days' notice of cancellation | The City as additional insured, at least as broad as ISO CG 20 26 |

Three takeaways from those documents:

1. **$1 million per occurrence and $2 million aggregate is the floor, not the ceiling.** Convention centers and public spaces often add an umbrella or excess layer on top.
2. **The additional insured list is usually more than one name.** Office buildings commonly want the owner, the property manager and the management company named separately, each with its exact legal entity name.
3. **Equipment matters.** A coffee cart is equipment brought into the building, and some office specs ask vendors who bring equipment to carry property coverage too. Read the whole spec, not only the liability lines.

## How to read the COI in five minutes

Wellesley College's [guide to reviewing ACORD certificates](https://www1.wellesley.edu/sites/default/files/assets/certificates_how_to_read_and_review_with_acord_forms.pdf) is one of the clearest walk-throughs online. Adapted for a coffee cart certificate of insurance on a one-day booking, check these boxes in order:

- [ ] **Date at the top** is recent. The Wellesley guide notes that missing or old dates can be a sign of a forged certificate.
- [ ] **Producer** is a real agency, and the certificate came from them or with their contact details.
- [ ] **Insured** matches the company on your contract and invoice.
- [ ] **Policy effective and expiration dates** cover your event date, including setup the morning of.
- [ ] **Limits** meet or beat every line in your building's spec: each occurrence, general aggregate, products-completed operations, auto, umbrella, workers comp.
- [ ] **ADDL INSD** and **SUBR WVD** boxes are marked on the lines where your spec requires them.
- [ ] **Description of Operations** names the event, date and location if your building asks, and lists every additional insured with exact legal names.
- [ ] **Certificate Holder** is the contracting entity your building specified, not a department or a person.
- [ ] **Endorsement copies** are attached if your property manager requires them.

If anything is off, send the caterer the specific correction ("add Meridian Management LLC as additional insured on general liability and umbrella") rather than forwarding the whole spec again. A precise request is easier for the agent to fix in one pass.

## Timeline: when to request the COI

A coffee cart certificate of insurance usually goes wrong when it is requested the day before. Build them into the booking.

| When | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| 4 to 6 weeks out | Book the coffee cart. Ask your property manager or venue for the written COI requirements and any vendor registration forms |
| Same day you book | Send the spec, the exact entity names and the event date and address to the caterer |
| 2 to 3 weeks out | Receive the certificate and check it against the checklist above. Send corrections |
| 1 to 2 weeks out | Submit the approved COI to property management. Ask them to confirm receipt in writing |
| 3 to 5 days out | Confirm the load-in route, elevator booking and security list for the crew |
| Event day | Keep a PDF of the approved certificate on your phone for the loading dock or lobby desk |

Busy months make this tighter: May, June, September, October and December fill fastest for coffee catering, so start the paperwork the day you book.

## COI requirements by setting

The certificate is the same document everywhere, but who asks for it, and what else they ask for, changes with the venue.

### Managed office towers

Class A buildings in Center City Philadelphia, Jersey City's Newport waterfront, DC's K Street corridor and Midtown Manhattan usually route vendors through property management. Expect the owner, manager and management company as additional insureds, plus a freight or service elevator booking. A compact cart that fits a standard passenger elevator helps when the freight elevator is booked, but it does not replace the COI.

### Hotels and conference venues

Hotels and convention centers often have exclusive caterers and their own vendor insurance rules. Confirm in writing that an outside coffee vendor is allowed at all before you collect a certificate. Our [conference coffee catering guide for DC](/posts/conference-coffee-catering-dc-convention-center/) covers how that works at a large convention center.

### Corporate and pharma campuses

Campuses add procurement on top of insurance: supplier forms, a W-9, vendor registration with security and a staff manifest. Our guide to [pharma meeting coffee in Philadelphia](/posts/pharma-coffee-catering-philadelphia/) has a full vendor onboarding checklist.

### Federal buildings and secure venues

In Washington DC, federal buildings and secure sites can need clearance paperwork and named staff lists well in advance, on top of the certificate. Our [Washington DC coffee cart catering guide](/posts/coffee-cart-catering-washington-dc/) covers early-morning setups and secure-building timing.

### Parks, plazas and sidewalks

Outdoor activations usually involve a public agency or a district manager, like Center City District in Philadelphia or a city transportation department in New York. These often require the city or district itself as an additional insured, on top of your permit. See our [Philadelphia coffee cart catering guide](/posts/coffee-cart-catering-philadelphia/) for outdoor plazas and neighborhoods.

## Questions to ask a coffee caterer about insurance

Before you sign, ask:

1. Can you issue a certificate of insurance to our building's written spec, including additional insured wording?
2. Can you provide the additional insured and waiver of subrogation endorsements if our property manager asks for them?
3. Do you carry auto, workers compensation and umbrella coverage, and at what limits?
4. Who is your insurance agent, and who on your team handles COI requests?
5. Can you also handle vendor registration and a staff manifest for our security desk?

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a certificate of insurance for a coffee cart?

It is a one-page summary, usually an ACORD 25 form, issued by the caterer's insurance agent. It lists the caterer's liability policies, policy numbers, dates and limits, and names your building or company as certificate holder. It is proof of coverage, not the policy itself.

### Is being the certificate holder the same as being an additional insured?

No. A certificate holder simply receives the certificate. Additional insured status has to be added to the caterer's policy by endorsement, so ask for the ADDL INSD box to be checked and for a copy of the endorsement if your building requires it.

### What insurance limits do buildings usually ask a coffee caterer for?

Many venues ask for $1,000,000 per occurrence and $2,000,000 aggregate in general liability, plus auto, workers compensation and sometimes an umbrella. Events DC and Philadelphia's Center City District both publish requirements at or above that level. Your building's written spec is what counts.

### How far ahead should I request a COI from a coffee cart near me?

Send your building's COI requirements the day you book, and aim to have the approved certificate in hand at least a week before the event. Book the cart itself four to six weeks ahead, since buildings and caterers both need time for paperwork.

### Can a coffee cart serve in my office building without a COI?

Rarely. Most managed office buildings, hotels, campuses and public venues will not let a vendor load in until property management has received and approved a certificate. Ask your property manager for the exact wording before you book.
