Search for conference coffee catering DC and the trail leads to one building: the Walter E. Washington Convention Center at 801 Allen Y. Lew Place NW, in the Mount Vernon Square area. It is also the building where planners most often get caught out, because the question is not which coffee vendor you like. It is who is allowed to pour coffee inside at all. This guide explains the rules first, then the options, the paperwork and the math, for exhibitors, sponsors and conference planners.

**The short answer:** Aramark is the convention center's exclusive food and beverage provider. Booth coffee is ordered from Aramark's exhibitor menu, and an outside coffee cart needs Aramark's approval, which may come with corkage fees. Ask in writing before you book anyone, and plan branded carts for the events around the show, where outside vendors fit easily.

> **About this post:** The Coffee Carts and Atelier Coffee Co. share an owner. This is a planning guide; Atelier does not claim to have served events at the Walter E. Washington Convention Center.

## Who pours coffee at the Walter E. Washington Convention Center

Events DC, which runs the center, says its food and beverage is provided by Aramark, its "exclusive premier catering partner," under the DC Eats name, on its [food and beverage page](https://eventsdc.com/venue/walter-e-washington-convention-center/food-beverage). Aramark became the exclusive partner in 2019 after a bid process, according to [Events DC's announcement](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/events-dc-announces-new-exclusive-food-and-beverage-partnership-with-globally-recognized-aramark-at-the-walter-e-washington-convention-center-300780279.html).

What that means for coffee, based on a [2024 Aramark exhibitor menu for the center](https://marinemilitaryexpos.com/wp-content/uploads/Aramark-2024-Exhibitor-Menu.pdf) posted by a show organizer:

- Aramark describes itself as the exclusive provider of all food and beverage in the building.
- Requests to bring in outside food or beverage are at Aramark's discretion and reviewed case by case.
- Corkage fees apply when an outside request is approved.
- Aramark's own exhibitor options include brewed coffee, Keurig service and a cappuccino and latte station.

Menus and terms change by year and by show, so treat this as the shape of the rules, not a quote. Your show's exhibitor service kit and Aramark's catering sales team have the current version.

## Three ways to handle conference coffee catering in DC

There is no single right answer. It depends on whether your coffee has to happen inside the exhibit hall, and how much the brand experience matters.

| Option | Where it works | What you control | What to watch |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Aramark booth or break service | Inside the convention center | Quantity, timing, placement in your booth | Ordering deadlines, fees, power and a counter you supply |
| Approved outside espresso cart | Inside, only if Aramark and show management approve | Menu, baristas, branding, cart design | Written approval, corkage, COI, floor plan, power order |
| Branded cart at events around the show | Your office, a nearby venue, a pre-show breakfast or reception | Everything: menu, wrap, cups, logo on foam | Each venue's own catering rules and building paperwork |

### Option 1: order booth coffee from Aramark

For a single exhibitor who wants coffee in the booth, the house caterer is the default path. Aramark's 2024 exhibitor menu asks the exhibitor to provide the hospitality counter or skirted table, and two 120-volt, 20-amp lines for the cappuccino station. Aramark does not supply tables or electrical hookups, so those go on your other service orders. The same menu sets written food and beverage specifications no less than 21 business days before first service, so this is not a last-week decision.

### Option 2: ask for an approved outside cart

If your sponsorship depends on a specific experience (a wrapped cart, a signature drink, your logo printed on the foam), you can ask. Go through show management and Aramark at the same time, explain what will be served and where, and get the answer and any corkage terms in writing. If the answer is no, you have still saved yourself a deposit.

### Option 3: put the branded cart where you set the rules

Much of a big DC conference happens outside the halls: the team breakfast before doors open, the client meeting in your K Street office, the recruiting session, the evening reception. Those events are where an outside cart is simplest, because you choose the venue. Downtown, Penn Quarter, Mount Vernon Triangle and the K Street corridor are all close to the center. Each venue still has its own rules, so check hotel and event-space catering policies the same way you would at the convention center.

## How to request an outside coffee cart, step by step

If you want an outside cart to be part of your conference coffee catering DC plan, run it like any other exhibitor service, with more lead time:

1. **Read the exhibitor service kit** for your show, including the food and beverage section and any sampling rules.
2. **Email show management and Aramark catering sales together.** Describe the cart, the menu, the footprint (Atelier's cart needs about 8 x 8 feet including barista space) and the service hours.
3. **Get approval and any corkage fee in writing** before you sign with a caterer.
4. **Send the caterer the venue's insurance requirements.** Atelier is fully insured and issues certificates of insurance to venue specs, including additional-insured language.
5. **Put the cart on the floor plan.** Events DC's [exhibitor services page](https://eventsdc.com/venue/walter-e-washington-convention-center/exhibitor-services) says floor plans must show food cart and equipment locations and be approved by the Event Manager and DC Fire, with plans due at least 31 days before move-in to avoid expedited fees.
6. **Order power from Hi-Tech Electric.** Events DC's [service partners page](https://eventsdc.com/venue/walter-e-washington-convention-center/service-partners) lists Hi-Tech Electric as the center's exclusive electrical and plumbing contractor. A dedicated outlet is ideal for an espresso cart; Atelier brings its own water, so you do not need a plumbing hookup.
7. **Confirm the load-in path** with the general service contractor (next section).

## Load-in at the convention center

The center is built for trade show freight, not for a coffee crew walking through a lobby, so plan the arrival like an exhibitor:

| Item | What Events DC says |
| --- | --- |
| Docks for Halls A, B, C | Enter at 7th and M Streets |
| Docks for Halls D, E | Enter at 9th and M Streets |
| Standard dock hours | 6am to 9pm daily |
| Event freight | Not accepted by the center; it is consigned to the show's general service contractor |
| Hand-carry entrance | 9th Street and Mount Vernon Place, for items carried in one trip |

Source: [Events DC exhibitor services](https://eventsdc.com/venue/walter-e-washington-convention-center/exhibitor-services).

Atelier's cart is compact and collapsible and fits a standard passenger elevator, which helps whenever freight elevators are booked for move-in. Timing matters too. In DC, an 8am service usually means Atelier's crew is on site by 6:30am, and the convention center's standard dock hours start at 6am, so confirm early access with the show if your service starts at doors open.

## Break-time math for a convention crowd

Conference coffee demand is lumpy: quiet during sessions, then a wave the moment a keynote ends. Plan for the wave. Each Atelier cart makes 65 to 75 specialty drinks an hour, which works out to:

| Break length | Drinks per cart | Two carts | Three carts |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 15 minutes | About 16 to 19 | About 32 to 38 | About 49 to 56 |
| 30 minutes | About 32 to 37 | About 65 to 75 | About 97 to 112 |
| 60 minutes | 65 to 75 | 130 to 150 | 195 to 225 |

These are planning estimates, not a promise for a specific event. Two ways to stretch capacity without more carts: run service through a longer window (an open-all-morning coffee bar instead of a single break), or add a second barista to a cart. For a booth, remember you do not need to serve the whole show, just the people you want to talk to. A coffee line of 15 people is 15 conversations.

## Branding that still works when the house caterer pours

If you end up with house coffee in the booth, the brand work moves to everything around the cup: booth signage, the counter, the moment a staffer hands over the drink. If your outside cart is approved, or you are hosting events around the show, Atelier's branding options stack up from light to heavy:

- **Logo on foam:** a latte art printer prints your logo on each drink.
- **Branded cups or printed sleeves:** the logo keeps walking the expo floor after the foam is gone.
- **Branded bar:** your logo on the cart.
- **Full cart wrap:** a wrapped cart with a custom menu and branded cups.
- **Signature drinks:** built on Atelier's homemade syrups such as Honey Lavender, Butter Brown Sugar or Spiced Brown Sugar.

### How do I order coffee for my booth at the DC convention center?

Order through Aramark's exhibitor catering for your show. Its 2024 exhibitor menu lists brewed coffee, Keurig service and a cappuccino and latte station. You supply the counter, and you order electrical service from Hi-Tech Electric, the center's exclusive electrical contractor.

### How many coffee carts do I need for a conference break in DC?

Work from your busiest break. One Atelier cart makes 65 to 75 specialty drinks an hour, or roughly 16 to 19 drinks in a 15-minute break. If 100 people will want coffee in the same 15 minutes, plan several carts or spread the demand across more of the day.
