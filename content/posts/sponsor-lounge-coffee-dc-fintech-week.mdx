Sponsor lounge coffee is one of the few conference sponsorships attendees seek out on their own. Nobody walks across an expo hall for a tote bag, but plenty of people will for a good latte at 10am. The catch is the line: a lounge with one overloaded cart turns a sponsorship into a 20-minute wait with your logo above it. This case study looks at how Atelier Coffee Co. set up three branded carts at DC Fintech Week, and what sponsors and conference planners can take from it.

**The short answer:** a sponsor lounge coffee bar works when the line stays short and the brand stays visible. Size service for the busiest break (each cart makes 65 to 75 specialty drinks an hour), split the crowd across more than one cart when a break is tight, and put the logo on the foam, the cups and the cart so it travels with every drink.

## The event: DC Fintech Week at National Landing

DC Fintech Week took place at National Landing in Arlington, Virginia, the area around Amazon's HQ2, in both 2025 and 2026. Atelier ran the coffee both years with the same setup:

| Element | What Atelier did |
| --- | --- |
| Carts | Three branded carts |
| Staffing | One barista per cart |
| Menus | A separate menu at each cart |
| Branding | The sponsor's logo printed on the foam of drinks |
| Location | National Landing, Arlington VA (Amazon HQ2 area) |
| Years | 2025 and 2026 |

That is the whole factual record, and it is enough to learn from. The interesting decision is not the coffee itself. It is choosing three separate stations, each with its own person and its own menu, instead of one big bar.

## Why three carts beat one big bar for sponsor lounge coffee

### Short lines are the sponsorship

A sponsor lounge has a strange traffic pattern. It is empty during sessions and slammed for the 15 to 30 minutes after each one ends. A single cart making 65 to 75 drinks an hour can serve roughly 16 to 19 people in a 15-minute break. Three carts can serve roughly 49 to 56 in the same window. The total hours did not change; the capacity at the moment it mattered tripled.

That matters for the sponsor because the line is where the impression forms. A guest who waits two minutes and chats with a barista remembers the brand fondly. A guest who gives up after ten minutes remembers the line.

### Three menus give people a reason to come back

Giving each cart its own menu turns the lounge into something to explore. An attendee who had a latte at one cart in the morning has a reason to try another in the afternoon, which means a second visit to the sponsor's space. If you plan your own lounge this way, a simple split works well:

- **Cart one, the classics:** espresso, latte, cappuccino, cold brew.
- **Cart two, the house syrups:** drinks built on Atelier's homemade syrups such as Honey Lavender, Butter Brown Sugar or Spiced Brown Sugar.
- **Cart three, the alternative:** a matcha bar with Rocky's Matcha ceremonial-grade matcha, or Turkish coffee brewed in a cezve.

The menus above are options Atelier offers, not the exact menus served at DC Fintech Week.

### Spreading stations spreads the crowd

Three stations in different spots also pull people through more of the room. For a sponsor, that is more square footage that feels busy. For the conference planner, it is less crowding at a single choke point near the session doors.

## How branding worked: logo on the foam

The DC Fintech Week carts used a latte art printer to put the sponsor's logo on the foam of drinks. Printed foam does something a banner cannot: it puts the logo in the attendee's hand, at the moment they are most likely to take a photo.

If you are planning sponsor lounge coffee, think of branding in layers, from lightest to heaviest:

| Layer | What it is | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Logo on foam | Latte art printer prints your logo on each drink | Photo moments, every sponsor level |
| Branded cups or printed sleeves | Your logo on the cup itself | Visibility after the guest leaves the lounge |
| Branded bar | Logo on the cart | Sponsor signage that doubles as wayfinding |
| Full cart wrap | Wrapped cart with a custom menu and branded cups | Title sponsors and brand-heavy lounges |
| Signature drink | A custom drink named for the sponsor or event | Giving people something to talk about |

Foam printing only lasts until the first sip, so pair it with cups or sleeves if you want the logo to keep walking around the expo hall. For the full playbook on wraps, cups and naming, see our guide to [coffee carts for corporate events](/posts/coffee-carts-for-corporate-events/).

## Lessons for sponsors planning a coffee lounge

### 1. Size for the busiest break, not the day

Take the attendance you expect in the lounge during the biggest break, then divide by how many drinks one cart can make in that window. At 65 to 75 drinks an hour per cart, a 30-minute break gives each cart about 32 to 37 drinks. If you expect 100 people in that window, one cart will not keep up.

| People in the lounge at peak (30 min) | Suggested setup |
| --- | --- |
| Up to about 35 | One cart |
| About 35 to 70 | Two carts, or one cart with a second barista |
| About 70 to 110 | Three carts, as at DC Fintech Week |
| More than 110 | Add carts or stagger break times with the organizer |

These are planning estimates based on Atelier's stated 65 to 75 drinks per cart per hour, not a promise for any specific event. Atelier has served events from 50-person offsites to 500-guest expo floors.

### 2. Settle venue rules before you design the lounge

Before you pick cup colors, ask the show organizer two questions in writing: can an outside coffee vendor serve in the sponsor area, and what does the venue need from that vendor? Some venues have an exclusive food and beverage provider, and the exhibitor manual is where you will find out. Once you have the answer, send the caterer the venue's insurance requirements. Atelier is fully insured and issues certificates of insurance to venue specs, including additional-insured language.

### 3. Get the footprint and power on the floor plan

Each cart needs about 8 x 8 feet including barista space. A dedicated outlet is ideal, and the cart brings its own water, so you do not need a plumbing hookup. If power in your lounge is limited, agree on a power plan in advance rather than discovering the problem at load-in. Three carts means three footprints and three power drops on the floor plan, so add them early.

### 4. Plan the load-in like any other exhibitor

Atelier's cart is compact and collapsible and fits a standard passenger elevator, which helps at venues without a free freight elevator or loading dock during move-in. For a morning opening, expect the crew on site well before service. In the DMV, an 8am start usually means arriving around 6:30am. Setup and breakdown are included in Atelier's bookings rather than billed separately.

### 5. Brief every barista on the sponsor

With one barista per cart, each one is a brand ambassador for a few hours. Give them two or three plain sentences about the sponsor and what you want guests to know. A barista who can answer "who's buying the coffee?" turns a drink into a conversation.

### 6. Book early in conference season

Book four to six weeks ahead. May, June, September, October and December fill fastest, and multi-cart setups need more lead time than a single cart because they tie up more crew and equipment on the same day.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many coffee carts does a sponsor lounge need?

Size the bar for your busiest break, not the whole day. One cart makes 65 to 75 specialty drinks an hour, so two carts handle roughly 130 to 150 and three carts roughly 195 to 225. If most attendees arrive in the same 30 minutes, add carts rather than hours.

### Can a sponsor's logo be printed on the coffee?

Yes. A latte art printer prints a logo on the foam of each drink. Pair it with branded cups or printed sleeves so the logo stays visible after the foam is gone.

### What does a coffee cart need in a conference sponsor lounge?

Plan about 8 x 8 feet per cart including barista space and a dedicated outlet if possible. Atelier's cart brings its own water, collapses to fit a standard passenger elevator, and limited power is usually workable with a power plan agreed in advance.

### Can I bring an outside coffee cart into a conference venue?

It depends on the venue and the show. Some venues have an exclusive food and beverage provider, so read the exhibitor manual and ask the show organizer in writing before you book an outside vendor.
