Coffee catering for government events is two problems stacked on top of each other. The first is the one every planner expects: getting an espresso cart and its baristas through a security desk, into a building and pouring before the first session. The second catches people out: whether anyone is allowed to pay for the coffee at all, and whether a contractor's coffee counts as a gift.

**The short answer:** before you book coffee catering for a government event, confirm who is paying and under what authority, because agencies generally can't buy food for their own staff at their duty station. Then send the caterer the building's security rules, ID requirements and staff manifest format four to six weeks out, and plan for an early crew arrival.

This guide is for agency event staff, government contractors, association planners and executive assistants in Washington DC. It is general information, not legal advice: your agency's budget office, counsel or ethics official has the final word.

## Coffee catering for government events: who pays

The funding source decides what you can serve, so settle it before you collect quotes. Here is how the common DC scenarios break down.

| Who is hosting | Who pays | The rule to check | Who to ask |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A federal agency, for its own staff | Agency appropriations | GAO's limits on feeding employees at their duty station | Budget office or appropriations counsel |
| A federal agency, at an official conference | Agency appropriations | The Federal Travel Regulation's conference provisions and your agency's own policy | Budget office, conference approval official |
| A contractor or company, with federal guests | The company | Federal ethics gift rules for the guests | The guests' agency ethics official |
| An association or industry conference | Sponsors or registration fees | Gift rules, including the widely attended gatherings provision | Attendees' ethics officials |
| An embassy or diplomatic event | The host mission | The host's own protocol | The host's protocol office |

### When an agency hosts its own staff

The baseline comes from the Government Accountability Office. In decision [B-288266 (January 27, 2003)](https://govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GAOREPORTS-B-288266/html/GAOREPORTS-B-288266.htm), GAO restated the general rule that, absent statutory authority, appropriated funds "are not available to feed government employees" at their duty station. Exceptions come from specific statutes, such as some training arrangements.

That makes a morning coffee bar for an all-hands or a team offsite harder to fund with appropriated dollars than most private-sector planners assume.

### When an agency runs an official conference

The Federal Travel Regulation, at [41 CFR 301-74.7](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/41/301-74.7), says agencies sponsoring a conference may provide light refreshments to employees attending it, and its list of light refreshments includes coffee and tea. But the same GAO decision above concluded that certifying officers should not rely on that provision for employees who are not in travel status. Agencies also set their own, sometimes stricter, policies.

[HHS's policy on spending for food](https://www.hhs.gov/grants/contracts/contract-policies-regulations/spending-on-food/index.html) is a good example. It bars its divisions from using appropriated funds for light refreshments or beverages unless one of four exceptions applies (including certain training events and award ceremonies), and requires a written justification memo approved at a senior level before food is bought.

The takeaway for a planner: get the funding answer in writing before you request a coffee cart quote, and file it with the purchase.

### When a contractor or company hosts federal guests

This is the most common DC case for corporate coffee catering: a government contractor's client day, a capability briefing at a Rosslyn or K Street office, or a sponsor lounge at an industry event. The money is private, so the question shifts to whether the coffee is a gift to the federal employees who drink it.

The executive branch ethics rules give a clear starting point. [5 CFR 2635.203(b)(1)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/5/2635.203) excludes "modest items of food and non-alcoholic refreshments," such as coffee and donuts, offered other than as part of a meal, from the definition of a gift. A coffee bar at a briefing generally sits inside that idea.

When hospitality goes beyond modest refreshments, other limits apply. Under [5 CFR 2635.204](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-5/chapter-XVI/subchapter-B/part-2635/subpart-B/section-2635.204), an employee may generally accept unsolicited gifts worth $20 or less per source per occasion, up to $50 a year from one source, and free attendance at widely attended gatherings requires a written determination by the agency. A branded keepsake mug or a full breakfast spread is where you should check first.

**Practical tip:** keep the coffee bar clearly part of the event, not a takeaway gift. Ask your federal guests' point of contact whether their ethics office has any concerns, especially if the guests are working on a procurement your company is pursuing.

## Getting baristas through federal building security

Once the funding is settled, the logistics follow a pattern that DC facilities teams will recognize. Downtown, Federal Triangle, Southwest DC and Capitol Hill buildings each run their own visitor process, so treat the list below as questions to ask rather than rules that apply everywhere.

### The paperwork checklist

- [ ] **Sponsor:** name the federal employee or office sponsoring the vendor's entry
- [ ] **Staff manifest:** full legal names of every barista, in the building's format, by its deadline
- [ ] **ID:** confirm what each person must show; names on IDs must match the manifest exactly
- [ ] **Clearance or background check:** ask whether vendor staff need one and how long it takes
- [ ] **Escort:** find out whether the crew needs an escort from the door to the room
- [ ] **Equipment screening:** ask whether the cart, grinder and supplies go through screening, and where
- [ ] **COI:** request the certificate of insurance wording, including any additional-insured language
- [ ] **Vehicle:** confirm where the van can stop and whether vehicle access needs its own approval
- [ ] **Entry time:** get the earliest time the crew can enter, in writing

On ID specifically: the [NIH Office of Research Services](https://ors.od.nih.gov/News/Pages/National-REAL-ID-Enforcement-Begins-May-7-2025.aspx) notes that since May 7, 2025, visitors using a driver's license to access federal facilities must use a REAL ID-compliant license, the kind with a star in the upper right-hand corner. Ask your caterer to confirm every barista on the manifest has acceptable ID, and to tell you early if a crew member changes.

### Why manifests break and how to avoid it

Most failed entries come down to small mismatches: a nickname on the manifest, a substitute barista added the night before, or a list sent to the wrong security office. Lock the crew a few days out, ask the caterer to send names exactly as they appear on ID, and give the security desk a phone number for your on-site contact.

## A timeline for coffee catering at a federal building

Work backward from the event date. Buildings with longer clearance processes need the early steps moved earlier.

| When | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| 6+ weeks out | Confirm the funding authority or ethics answer in writing |
| 4 to 6 weeks out | Request quotes; send headcount, hours, building and security rules |
| 3 to 4 weeks out | Book; send COI wording and the manifest template |
| 1 to 2 weeks out | Caterer submits the manifest and any clearance forms |
| 3 to 5 days out | Lock the crew; confirm entrance, elevator, escort and entry time |
| Event morning | Crew checks in, loads in and sets up; for an 8am service Atelier is usually on site by 6:30am |

Many federal buildings open their doors on a fixed schedule. If the crew cannot enter until 7am, tell the caterer, and shift the first pour to a break rather than squeezing a rushed setup into the welcome.

## Sizing the bar for a government audience

Coffee catering for government events usually has one hard peak: the 30 minutes before a briefing, hearing-style session or all-hands starts. Each Atelier cart makes 65 to 75 specialty drinks per hour, so plan for that peak rather than the day's average.

| Expected guests | What the first half hour looks like | Setup to consider |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Up to 60 | One steady line | One cart, one barista |
| 60 to 150 | Line builds before the session | One cart, add a second barista |
| 150 to 300 | Too much for one bar in the rush | Two carts, or a cart plus a second station |
| 300+ | Arrivals spread across entrances | Multiple carts near each entrance or lobby |

If the budget is tight or the rules limit spending, a shorter window works: open 45 minutes before the first session, close at the start, and reopen for a single break.

## Space, power and building rules

Federal buildings and secure offices rarely allow last-minute improvisation, so share the footprint up front.

- **Footprint:** about 8 by 8 feet, including barista space
- **Power:** a dedicated outlet is ideal; limited power is usually workable with a plan agreed in advance
- **Water:** none needed from the building, because Atelier brings its own
- **Access:** Atelier's compact, collapsible cart fits a standard passenger elevator, which matters when a freight elevator or loading dock is off limits to outside vendors

Ask facilities whether open flames, extension cords or particular cleaning products are restricted, and whether the room has a floor covering requirement. Those answers shape what the caterer brings.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a federal agency pay for coffee at its own event?

Often not. GAO's general rule is that appropriated funds are not available to feed employees at their duty station without specific authority. Exceptions exist, such as some training and award ceremonies, so ask your budget office before you book.

### Can a government contractor serve coffee to federal employees?

Usually, if it stays modest. Federal ethics rules exclude modest refreshments such as coffee and donuts, offered other than as part of a meal, from the definition of a gift. Bigger hospitality falls under other limits, so check with the agency's ethics official.

### What ID do baristas need to enter a federal building in DC?

Whatever the building's security office asks for, and the names must match the staff manifest. If a driver's license is used, federal facilities have required a REAL ID-compliant license since May 7, 2025.

### How far ahead should I book coffee catering for a federal building?

Four to six weeks is a sensible minimum, and more if the building needs clearance paperwork for vendor staff. Send the security requirements with your first quote request.
